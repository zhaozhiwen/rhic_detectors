## Abstract (paraphrase)

The paper gives details of the design, construction and performance of the STAR Barrel Electromagnetic Calorimeter.

---

## Full content

### 1. Introduction

The BEMC is one of the first major upgrades to the STAR baseline detector. It is installed in stages: the first ≈25% was to be installed and ready for the **2001 run**, and the rest at about 25% per year. STAR is nominally a slow detector; the BEMC lets it **trigger on** and study rare high-$P_T$ processes (jets, leading hadrons, direct photons, heavy quarks), and gives large acceptance for photons, electrons, π⁰ and η mesons in systems from polarized p+p to Au+Au. Other uses include general event characterization in heavy-ion collisions, including ultra-peripheral collisions.

The paper first surveys the technology and layout, then the subsystems, and closes with examples of performance.

### 2. Technology choices

**Area and depth.** The area to be covered outside the STAR TPC is **over 60 m²** for |η| < 1, so cost drives the technology choice. To contain electromagnetic showers up to **60 GeV** and fit the mechanical constraints of STAR, the calorimeter has a total depth of about **20 radiation lengths (20 X₀) at η = 0**.

**[note]** Area check: a cylinder of radius 2.235 m (front face, Fig. 4) and length 2 × 2.235 × sinh(1) = 5.25 m has 2π × 2.235 × 5.25 ≈ **73.8 m²** ✓ (> 60 m²).

**Pb/scintillator sampling.** Because of the large area and complex geometry, a lead/plastic-scintillator sampling calorimeter was chosen. It can be built from many fairly small modules, so it could be built and installed after the baseline components (TPC, magnet, etc.) were finished. The technology is well established in colliding-beam and fixed-target experiments [1] and is a cost-effective way to cover the area with a mechanical design flexible enough for the closed STAR geometry.

**Light transport.** With PMT readout and the limited space inside the STAR magnet, all scintillation light must be carried outside the magnet steel, where there is room for the phototubes, bases, HV system and front-end electronics. This complicates the optics, but the PMTs then sit in a near-zero magnetic field, which reduces PMT system cost and complexity. Wavelength-shifter plates and bars (as in CDF and ZEUS) would take too much space where they pass the coil pancakes and iron flux return of the large room-temperature solenoid. So, following CDF [2], STAR uses **plastic WLS fibres plus clear optical fibres**, which take much less space and much less labour.

**Towers and SMD.** With such a large area it is impractical to make towers as small as the Molière radius of the Pb/scintillator composite. Yet the physics needs reconstruction of π⁰'s and isolated (direct) photons up to $P_T$ ≈ 25–30 GeV/c, and identification of single electrons and pairs in large hadron backgrounds (heavy vector mesons, W and Z decays). This needs high spatial resolution of the shower. STAR therefore puts **shower-maximum detectors** (essentially two layers of gas wire/pad chambers) inside the Pb/scintillator stack, measuring the shower in two orthogonal transverse directions. Tower size then needs only to give reasonable occupancy in typical events.

**Preshower.** Even the best shower position resolution struggles to separate π⁰'s from γ's at $P_T$ as high as ≈25 GeV/c at STAR's radius. Therefore each tower also has an independent **preshower** readout, which samples longitudinal shower development after only **(1–1.5) X₀** and helps both π⁰/γ and e/h discrimination. At this depth electrons and charged hadrons deposit very different energy: an electron already has a much larger dE/dx before it showers, and **≈63%** of electrons shower before entering the active preshower volume and **≈84%** by the middle of the preshower. The corresponding hadron interaction probabilities are about **3%** and **6%**. So electron and hadron energy distributions in the preshower differ strongly, almost independently of energy.

**[note]** For electrons, P(shower) = 1 − exp(−7t/9) gives t ≈ 1.28 X₀ for 63% and t ≈ 2.36 X₀ for 84%; the difference, 1.08 X₀, is close to one 5 mm Pb layer (0.89 X₀) plus scintillator ✓. Since Sc1 sits directly behind the front plate (Fig. 4), the ≈1.3 X₀ before Sc1 must come mostly from material in front of the calorimeter. For hadrons, one 5 mm Pb layer gives 1 − exp(−0.5/17.6) = 2.8% ≈ 3% and two give 5.6% ≈ 6% (λ_I(Pb) = 17.6 cm, my value). So the hadron numbers count one Pb layer before "layer 1" (§8 says "considering only the Pb"), whereas Fig. 4 shows no Pb in front of Sc1. Small inconsistency in what "before layer 1" means; it does not affect the argument.

**Location and coverage.** The BEMC sits **inside the aluminum coil** of the STAR solenoid and covers **|η| ≤ 1.0** and **2π** in azimuth, matching full TPC tracking (Fig. 1). Its front face is at a radius of **≈220 cm**, parallel to the beam axis.

### 4. The STAR BEMC optical structure

**Scintillator.** There are **21 active scintillator layers** of **Kuraray SCSN81**: **19 of 5 mm** and **2 of 6 mm** (preshower). They alternate with **20 layers of 5 mm Pb**. The scintillator is machined as **"megatile" sheets**, each with **40 optically isolated tiles** per layer; Fig. 2 shows the 21st sheet. Each tile is read by a **WLS fibre in a "σ-groove"** machined into it. Tiles are isolated by cutting **95%** of the way through the sheet and backfilling with opaque **SiO₂-loaded epoxy**. Cross-talk through the remaining 5% is reduced to **< ½%** by a thin black line painted over the grooves on the uncut surface.

**[note]** The HTML prints "<12%" here and in criterion (iii) below; the rendered PDF shows "<½%" in both places. The PDF wins.

**Machining.** Each module needs **840 different tile shapes (420 plus their mirror images)**, cut on a dedicated high-speed router with two heads working in parallel, making two identical megatiles at once at linear speeds approaching **200 in./min**. **[note]** 21 layers × 20 η-towers = 420 shapes, ×2 for the φ mirror ✓.

**Reflectors.** The machined, unpolished megatile edges are painted white with **Bicron BC620**. **White bond paper** (good diffuse reflectivity and, importantly, high friction) lies on both faces of each megatile as a diffuse reflector between layers.

**Fibre routing.** After leaving the tiles, the WLS fibres run along the outside of the stack under the light-tight cover to a multi-fibre optical connector at the module's back plate (Fig. 3). A **2.1 m** multi-fibre cable of clear fibres, with mating connectors, carries the light through the magnet structure to **decoder boxes on the outer surface of the STAR magnet**. There the light from the **21 tiles of one tower is merged onto one PMT**.

**Light mixer.** A light mixer between fibres and PMT makes all 21 fibres illuminate a common area of the photocathode. It is clear Lucite, **2″ × 0.5″ × 0.5″**, a compromise between the housing space and the mixing function, based on Fermilab studies [3]. Those studies found the diffusion optimal for a square rod longer than 4 times its transverse size. With the mixer, the effect of photocathode quantum-efficiency non-uniformity on the layer-to-layer response drops to a few percent. Fig. 5 shows the tile/fibre readout scheme. **[note]** 2″/0.5″ = 4, i.e. exactly at, not above, the stated optimum ratio.

**Fibres.** WLS fibre: **Kuraray multiclad Y11 (200 ppm), S-type, 0.83 mm diameter**, one end polished and mirrored with sputtered aluminum. Clear fibre in the 2.1 m cables: **Kuraray double-clad 0.9 mm**. Inside the PMT decoder boxes: **Kuraray double-clad 1.0 mm**. At each junction the fibre diameter increases, to optimize light transfer through the connectors.

**PMTs.** **Electron Tube Inc. 9125B**: ≈**30 mm** diameter, **11 dynodes**, linear-focused, photocathode sensitive diameter ≈**23 mm**; typical peak QE near **25%**. Accepted tubes have a mean QE of **13.3%** (s.d. **1.3%**) at the wavelength delivered by the BEMC fibres; no tube with QE below **10% at 490 nm** is used. Further acceptance criteria: dark current **< 5 nA at gain 2 × 10⁵**, non-linearity **< 2% at 20 mA peak current**. The 9125B was chosen for linearity, dark current, gain and QE, and lower cost. All tubes are factory-tested before delivery and then tested again (gain, dark current, linearity) on an automated setup before installation.

**PMT base.** To meet linearity, noise, STAR safety and low-power requirements at lower cost, the group built an "intelligent" **Cockcroft–Walton base** (spelled "Cockroft Walton" in the paper) with a pulse-frequency/pulse-width-modulation system generating and regulating the HV inside the base. The HV division ratio, optimized for linearity, is **2:1:1:1:1:1:1:1:2:3:4:3 (K:d1:…:d11:A)**. Each base has an **8051-compatible flash microcontroller** (e.g. Analog Devices ADUC812) with internal **12-bit DAC and ADCs**. HV ripple at the photocathode with a **1 μA** load is **0.05% at 1000 V**. Noise injected into the anode signal is far below **0.15 pC (one LSB)**. The HV range is limited in hardware and firmware to **−1520 V**; setpoint resolution **0.6 V**, accuracy **0.3%** of full range.

**HV control.** The bases are controlled over an **RS485** serial network (master–slave). Each base has a unique **12-bit address**, so up to **4096** bases fit on one network segment. Because of RS485 transceiver limits the master (PC) signal is regenerated by repeaters; **each PMT box of 80 PMTs** has one repeater. The slow-control software is **LabView** on the master PC. The bases can also measure temperature and current, but current measurement is disabled in the latest hardware revision. More HV details were to be published separately. **[note]** 4800 towers / 80 PMTs per box = **60 PMT boxes**; 2¹² = 4096 ✓.

**Photostatistics and resolution.** Layer-by-layer tests and full-system tests with cosmic rays and test beams give on average **3 photoelectrons per mip per layer** through the full optical chain. With this photostatistics an ideal sampling calorimeter would have a resolution of

$$\frac{\sigma_E}{E} \approx \frac{14\%}{\sqrt{E}} \oplus 1.5\% \qquad \text{(unnumbered)}$$

(written in the text as "≈14%/√E plus a 1.5% constant term added in quadrature"; E in GeV)

In practice transverse and longitudinal non-uniformities within a tower and cross-talk between towers limit the resolution. The design and QA criteria ensure that:

1. (i) transverse uniformity of the light from each tile is better than **5% RMS**;
2. (ii) longitudinal uniformity is better than **10% RMS** within each tower, including all fibre-system variations;

### 5. Shower maximum detector

**Purpose.** The SMD gives fine spatial resolution in a calorimeter whose towers are much larger than an EM shower. Each of the 4800 towers spans **Δη × Δφ = 0.05 × 0.05**, i.e. **∼10 × 10 cm²** at η = 0 at the inner face, growing towards η = 1. The towers give precise energies for isolated EM showers, but the SMD's resolution is essential for π⁰ reconstruction, direct-γ identification and electron identification. It provides shower position and shape and, from the amplitude, information on longitudinal development.

**[note]** At r = 223.5 cm, 0.05 rad → 11.2 cm in rφ, and Δη = 0.05 → Δz = r cosh η Δη = 11.2 cm at η = 0 and 17.2 cm at η = 1 ✓ (∼10 × 10 cm² at η = 0).

**Depth and layout.** The SMD is at ≈**5.6 radiation lengths** at η = 0, counting all material immediately in front of the calorimeter. Footnote 1: counting only calorimeter material, the SMD depth goes from **4.6 X₀ to 7.1 X₀** as η goes from 0 to 1. Its special feature is a **double-layer design**: a two-sided aluminum extrusion forms ground channels for **two independent planes of proportional wires**, and separate PC-board cathode planes with strips etched along η and along φ give a two-dimensional image of the shower (Fig. 7).

**[note]** 4.6 X₀ × cosh(1) = 4.6 × 1.543 = 7.10 X₀ ✓. Before the SMD there are 5 Pb layers (4.45 X₀) plus 27 mm of scintillator (≈0.06 X₀) plus the front plate ≈ 4.6 X₀ ✓. So ≈1.0 X₀ of material lies in front of the BEMC at η = 0 (5.6 − 4.6).

The amplifier is essentially the **STAR TPC amplifier** with the ASIC modified to suit the larger SMD dynamic range. Shaping time and integrator reset time were tuned with real pulse shapes from radioactive sources. The combined SMD + FEE uniformity is expected to be about **±14%** before calibration, with channel-to-channel cross-talk **≤ 1%**.

### 6. Preshower detector

Every one of the 4800 towers has a preshower detector, formed by the **first and second scintillator layers**. Their fibre grooves are cut deeper and hold **two WLS fibres** each, so two samples of the light from these layers go to the PMT/decoder boxes. The first pair of fibres (layers 1 and 2) joins the **19 other tower fibres** to give the total-energy signal. The second pair illuminates **one pixel of a multi-anode PMT**; **300 sixteen-pixel MAPMTs** handle the **4800 fibre pairs**.

Two fibres in one groove each deliver **20% less light** than a single fibre. That does not matter for the preshower signal, but it would put layers 1 and 2 outside the **10%** longitudinal-uniformity tolerance for the tower. To compensate, layers 1 and 2 are **6 mm** thick.

**[note]** 300 × 16 = 4800 ✓. 2 + 19 = 21 layers per tower ✓. 6 mm/5 mm = 1.2, which roughly offsets the 20% loss (0.8 × 1.2 = 0.96) ✓.

### 7. Summary of BEMC electronics systems

The EMC electronics comprise trigger, PMT and SMD readout, PMT HV, low-voltage power, slow controls, calibration controls, and interfaces to STAR trigger, DAQ and slow controls. Most front-end electronics (signal processing, digitization, buffering, trigger-primitive formation, first readout level) is in **custom EMC crates outside the magnet iron**. The exception is the SMD preamplifiers and switched-capacitor arrays (the SMD analog pipeline), which are on the modules inside the magnet.

**Level-0.** In STAR's trigger hierarchy, level-0 is the detector information available without dead time at every RHIC crossing. Level-0 *selects* events; all higher levels only *abort* them. The BEMC uses **gated integrators and 12-bit flash ADCs** with a long digital pipeline, so it runs dead-timeless at the RHIC clock and is a key part of STAR level-0, able to trigger on high-$P_T$ physics via its EM component. For example, the STAR jet trigger relies mainly on leading π⁰'s in the BEMC, with some contribution from the much smaller energy of hadronic showers; more level-0 jet information comes from STAR's multiplicity detectors.

**Timing budget.** Level-0 is the only level without large dead time from opening the TPC gated grid; the grid cycling rate is limited, so as much as possible should be done at level-0. The EMC suits level-0 because it is fast and measures total energy. The level-0 trigger must reach the TPC within about **1 μs (∼10 RHIC crossings)** and the level-0 processors within about **700 ns**, including cable delays. **[note]** RHIC crossings are ≈106 ns apart, so 1 μs ≈ 9.4 crossings ✓.

**Trigger primitives.** For speed and bandwidth, the trigger uses primitives instead of full data. There are two kinds: **300 tower sums**, 6 bits each, from **0.2 × 0.2 patches** in η–φ; and **300 high-tower values**, 6 bits, giving the largest single **0.05 × 0.05** tower in each patch. These are processed into decisions on total $E_T$, jets, photons, etc., and passed to STAR level-0 within **700 ns** to form part of the level-0 decision. **[note]** A 0.2 × 0.2 patch is 4 × 4 = 16 towers; 4800/16 = 300 ✓.

**Tower data path.** PMT signals are integrated and digitized in the front-end cards **every RHIC crossing**, pipelined until the level-0 decision and, on a trigger, moved to a **token-addressable memory in the tower data collector** on the STAR electronics platform to await readout.

**SMD data path.** Strip signals are amplified by a simple transimpedance amplifier and driver on the front-end cards, then held in an analog switched-capacitor pipeline until level-0. On a level-0 trigger they are read out with **80:1 multiplexing** into the **10-bit SMD digitizers**. Digitized SMD data reach STAR's **level-2** trigger processors in **∼200 μs**, well before TPC data.

### 8. BEMC performance estimates

**Scope.** As an example, the paper studies **electron–hadron discrimination from 500 MeV to 5 GeV**, the range seen in Au+Au. STAR's large acceptance and event-by-event capability make it important for vector-meson production and suppression in heavy-ion collisions, if electrons can be identified and hadrons suppressed. For φ → e⁺e⁻ and J/ψ, the electrons sit on a much larger hadron background, so electron efficiency and hadron rejection set the reach for these and other lepton channels; these measurements bear directly on quark–gluon-plasma physics.

Low energies are chosen because they matter most for the heavy-ion programme and have the hardest hadron background. The BEMC is not limited to this range: it is the central tool for STAR spin physics, where electrons from W and Z decay and Drell–Yan pairs probe, among other things, sea-quark polarization in the proton.

The aim was better estimates of electron identification at **0.5–5.0 GeV**, relevant for J/ψ and perhaps φ → e⁺e⁻ at low $P_T$ in Au+Au. Electron–hadron discrimination was studied with data from a **1998 EMC test-beam run**, extended by simulations of the calorimeter including the preshower (PSD) and SMD. Other STAR detectors (TOF and RICH, and TPC dE/dx) can also help, but TOF and RICH will initially have too little acceptance for vector-meson work, although they extend PID for more abundant particles. TPC dE/dx will contribute substantially in the relativistic-rise region and below about **300–400 MeV**, where pions have β noticeably below one (**> 5% at 400 MeV**). This comes with full acceptance and can be combined with the BEMC's hadron rejection. In vector-meson invariant-mass spectra each additional independent hadron-suppression factor enters squared.

**[note]** For a 400 MeV/c pion, β = p/√(p² + m²) = 0.400/0.424 = 0.944, i.e. 5.6% below one ✓ (> 5%).

**Underlying event.** In a central Au+Au event the mean hadronic background per BEMC tower is about **140 MeV/tower**, with a standard deviation of **170 MeV**. For J/ψ a minimum electron energy of **1.5 GeV** is required, where the intrinsic resolution is **σ_E/E = 12% (∼180 MeV)**. The underlying event effectively degrades this to **17% at 1.5 GeV** for the most central events. At **3 GeV** the effect is smaller, degrading the resolution **from 9% to 10%**. Even modest isolation cuts with TPC tracking and the SMD can reduce the mean background well below these values (not discussed further).

**[note]** Recomputed with 14%/√E ⊕ 1.5%: 11.5% at 1.5 GeV ≈ 12% ✓, and 12% × 1.5 GeV = 180 MeV ✓; √(180² + 170²) = 248 MeV = 16.5% ≈ 17% ✓. At 3 GeV: 8.2% intrinsic (paper: 9%, ≈) → √(246² + 170²) = 299 MeV = 10.0% ✓.

**Eight discriminating variables.** The analysis considers all information from towers, SMD and PSD: $E/p$, $E_{\mathrm{SMD}}(\eta)$, $E_{\mathrm{SMD}}(\phi)$, $\Delta\eta$, $\Delta\phi$, $\sigma_\eta$, $\sigma_\phi$ and $E_{\mathrm{PSD}}$.

1. **E/p.** The tower energy is a high-resolution, linear measure of the full electron energy, while hadrons, even when they shower, usually deposit much less than their total energy. Comparing tower energy to the tracked momentum is thus an electron tag whose power depends on the TPC momentum resolution and the calorimeter energy resolution. The latter scales as ∝ 1/√E, so E/p selection improves with energy until TPC resolution dominates at high $P_T$.
2. **E_SMD (energy in the SMD).** The SMD is at ≈5.6 X₀ including the material in front. There it is near the shower maximum for EM showers above about **1–2 GeV**, whereas hadronic showers peak near one interaction length (e.g. **17 cm for Pb**) and are spread much more in depth. So the SMD energy relative to the tower energy suppresses hadrons. The EM shower-maximum depth grows logarithmically with energy, so this works over a wide range (> 1–2 GeV). Below **1 GeV** the shower maximum quickly moves in front of 5.6 X₀ and the SMD adds little. Hadrons that pass an SMD cut tend to have showered early, often with leading π⁰'s, and so deposit an above-average fraction of their energy — exactly those hardest to reject with E/p. Such correlations make combined detectors less effective than the product of individual rejections. The two SMD layers, $E_{\mathrm{SMD}}(\eta)$ and $E_{\mathrm{SMD}}(\phi)$, are strongly correlated on average but their instrumental fluctuations are much less so, and preliminary studies showed a clear gain from using both.
3. **Δη, Δφ (shower position).** TPC tracking predicts the impact point at the calorimeter to about a millimetre. The SMD reconstructs the η and φ positions from the centroids of the transverse shower profiles. Hadronic showers, usually not developed by 5.6 X₀, have centroids that fluctuate strongly relative to the extrapolated track. The residuals Δη, Δφ therefore add hadron rejection where SMD signals are good, typically > 1 GeV.
4. **σ_η, σ_φ (shower shape).** EM showers are compact: ∼**95%** of the energy lies within a cylinder of radius twice the Molière radius (e.g. **2R_M = 3.2 cm for Pb**). Fully developed hadronic showers are about one interaction length λ wide (at depth ∼λ). At the SMD depth, partially developed hadron showers fluctuate in width but can still be much wider. So the profile widths σ_η, σ_φ should help reject hadrons, again for electrons above about 1 GeV.
5. **E_PSD (preshower energy).** The first two scintillator layers form the PSD. An electron has a higher dE/dx before showering, and ∼**63%** of electrons shower before scintillator layer 1 and ∼**84%** before layer 2, against hadron interaction probabilities (Pb only) of about **3%** and **6%**. The distributions therefore differ almost independently of energy. The PSD is particularly important near **1.5 GeV**, where the SMD starts to lose power. Because few hadronic interaction lengths precede it, the PSD is largely uncorrelated with the other signals and gives a nearly constant hadron suppression at fixed electron efficiency.

**[note]** The paper writes "the low hadronic interaction lengths which proceed the preshower"; "precede" is meant.

**Neural network.** The signals are correlated to varying degrees, and the correlations change with energy, so they must be analysed together; the contribution of one signal is found by removing it and comparing. Hence a **neural-network** analysis: removing a signal (or group) and retraining gives its correlation-corrected contribution. As a first study, a **seven-node network** (E_SMD(η) and E_SMD(φ) not separated) was trained on **GSTAR**-simulated data to separate electrons from hadrons. Samples: pure **π⁺ or e⁺ at 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 and 5.0 GeV/c**, typically **1000 electrons** and **5000 hadrons**.

**Simulation details.** Tower and preshower ADC values were computed from the measured yield of **3 photoelectrons per MeV** deposited and smeared with a realistic single-photoelectron response. This matters most for the preshower, where a mip gives on average just **8 photoelectrons** from its two layers combined. Lacking a good full-system model of the SMD, GEANT energy depositions were used directly; electronic noise should degrade SMD performance only at the lowest energies, where the PSD matters more anyway. The particle momentum was smeared according to the expected TPC performance before forming a "measured" E/p. Fig. 9 shows the network output α at 1.0 GeV/c: hadrons peak near **α = 0**, electrons near **α = 1**.

**[note]** 3 pe per MeV agrees with "3 pe per mip per layer" in §4 if a mip deposits ≈1 MeV in 5 mm of scintillator (≈2 MeV/cm) ✓. For the PSD the same yield gives ≈1.2 MeV × 3 = 3.6 pe per 6 mm layer, ×0.8 for the shared groove ≈ 2.9 pe, i.e. ≈5.8 pe for two layers, somewhat below the stated 8 (≈; the paper does not show how 8 was obtained).

**Efficiencies.** From such distributions at each energy, the hadron efficiency $\varepsilon_h$ and electron efficiency $\varepsilon_e$ follow by integrating from a cut $\alpha_{\mathrm{cut}}$ to α = 1; varying $\alpha_{\mathrm{cut}}$ gives $\varepsilon_h$ as a function of $\varepsilon_e$. The hadron suppression factor $h = 1/\varepsilon_h$ versus electron efficiency is shown in Fig. 10 for 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 and 5.0 GeV/c. Roughly, the signal-to-background ratio for a single electron is $(M_e/M_h)(\varepsilon_e/\varepsilon_h)$, with $M_e$, $M_h$ the electron and hadron multiplicities.

### 9. Conclusions

The paper has described the STAR BEMC, among the first major upgrades to the STAR baseline, built and installed in stages with the first ∼25% ready for the 2001 run. With acceptance equal to that of the TPC for full-length tracks, it lets STAR trigger on and study rare high-$P_T$ processes (jets, leading hadrons, direct photons, heavy quarks) and gives large acceptance for photons, electrons, π⁰ and η mesons from polarized p+p to Au+Au.

**(c) Readout chain, electronics, trigger role**
- Tile → Y11 WLS (0.83 mm, mirrored) → 10-pin connector on back plate → 2.1 m clear-fibre cable (0.9 mm) → decoder box on magnet exterior (1.0 mm fibre) → 2″ Lucite mixer → one ETI 9125B PMT per tower (21 fibres).
- Cockcroft–Walton bases with ADUC812 microcontroller, RS485 control, LabView; 80 PMTs per box (→ 60 boxes [note]); HV limit −1520 V.
- Towers: gated integrators + 12-bit flash ADCs every crossing, digital pipeline, token-addressable memory in the tower data collector.
- SMD: TPC-type amplifier ASIC + SCA on the module (FEE at η = 1 end), 80:1 multiplexing to 10-bit digitizers outside the magnet; data at level-2 in ∼200 μs.
- Trigger: dead-timeless level-0 detector; 300 patch sums + 300 high towers (6 bits each) to level-0 in 700 ns; total $E_T$, jet, photon triggers; 120 SMD wire signals could give shower-max triggers.

**(d) Calibration and measured performance**
- 3 pe per mip per layer; ideal resolution ≈14%/√E ⊕ 1.5%. QA limits: tile transverse uniformity < 5% RMS, tower longitudinal uniformity < 10% RMS, cross-talk < ½%; measured tile-yield RMS 8.1% (160 tiles).
- PMT QE 13.3% ± 1.3% (s.d.) at the fibre wavelength, ≥ 10% at 490 nm; dark current < 5 nA at 2 × 10⁵; non-linearity < 2% at 20 mA.
- SMD (AGS test beam, 0.5–5 GeV): linear response; σ/E = 12% + 86%/√E (front, back 3–4% worse); σ_front = 2.4 + 5.6/√E mm, σ_back = 3.2 + 5.8/√E mm; SMD+FEE uniformity ±14% before calibration; cross-talk ≤ 1%.
- e/h separation: neural-network study (GSTAR, seven inputs) gives the h(ε_e) curves of Fig. 10; no absolute tower energy calibration method (e.g. mip or electron calibration in situ) is described in this paper.
- No measured tower energy resolution from a test beam is given; only the "ideal" estimate and the 12% at 1.5 GeV used in §8.

**(e) Rates, occupancy, radiation, ageing**
- SMD occupancy ≈1% (p+p), > 5 to ≈25% (Au+Au, threshold-dependent); SMD signal length 110 ns.
- Central Au+Au background: 140 MeV per tower mean, 170 MeV s.d.
- Coil ripple up to 400 V is the stated noise threat for the SMD.
- **Not covered:** radiation dose, ageing of scintillator/fibres, gas ageing, PMT rate limits.

**(f) Relevance to STAR TOF, MTD and calorimeters in this folder**
- **Barrel TOF:** the BEMC front face (r ≈ 220 cm text, 223.5 cm Fig. 4) sits just outside the TOF trays (radius ~220 cm in [[star-barrel-tof]]; Fig. 1 labels the TOF ring "CBT/TOF" between TPC and BEMC). The TOF must therefore fit below r = 223.5 cm, inside the BEMC. The BEMC module (6° in φ, 120 modules as 60 × 2) has the same φ segmentation as the BTOF trays (60 per ring × 2 rings) [note].
- **MTD (absorber in front):** a muon reaching the MTD crosses the BEMC (≈20 X₀; 100 mm Pb ≈ 0.57 λ_I, plus 107 mm scintillator, the Al SMD and steel/Al plates, ≈0.7 λ_I in total [note]), then the aluminum coil and the iron flux-return bars. The BEMC envelope ends at r = 2537.99 mm (back plate) and 2629.99 mm (rail track); [[star-magnet-absorber]] puts the backlegs at r = 363 cm, ≈61 cm thick, with the MTD at ~400–403 cm. This supports the open point (see [[star-magnet-absorber]], [[open-questions]]) that ≈5 λ needs "the BEMC and coil too": the BEMC adds ≈0.7 λ_I [note]. The magnet coil thickness is not given here (see the magnet paper, NIM A 499 (2003) 633).
- **Backlegs:** Fig. 1 shows the iron flux-return bars around the coil; the drawing appears consistent with 30 bars at 12° spacing (a bar centred at the top, a pair straddling the horizontal) — read from the figure, not stated in the text [note]. The BEMC fibres run between these bars, and the PMT decoder boxes (60 boxes of 80 PMTs [note]) sit on the outer surface of the magnet, i.e. in the same region as the MTD trays; services may compete for space there [note].
- **EEMC / FCS:** compared with the EEMC ([[star2003-eemc-note]]), the BEMC has a gas SMD at ≈5 X₀ (EEMC: scintillator strips) and a preshower from its first two tiles. Its ideal resolution ≈14%/√E ⊕ 1.5% compares with the EEMC requirement 16%/√E + 2%.
